## Problem Statement

The assignment needs a public promo page on GitHub Pages. The page has to make an international student in Budapest want to try the app, and show the teacher what the app actually does, without anything personal on it.

## What Makes This Cool

Your money arrives in one currency and Budapest charges you in another. Every tögrög, euro and dollar becomes forints at today's rate, and a whole bank statement in tögrög sorts itself into Food, Going out and Unknown, month by month. Most budget apps assume one currency.

## Constraints

- Only true claims. Live exchange rates are promised, so they are built before the page goes online (decided in this session: content first, then live rates).
- Nothing personal: no real budget numbers, no real statement, no account details. Only the made-up sample statement and the demo screenshots.
- The page lives in `docs/`, and Pages publishes only that folder. This design doc is kept in `design/`, not `docs/`, so it is not published.
- The look comes later from Impeccable (shape → generate → critique). This doc is content only.

## Premises (agreed)

1. Reader: international students in Budapest whose money arrives in tögrög, euros or dollars while rent, food and transport are paid in forints. The teacher and classmates read it too, but the page talks to the student.
2. Hook: every currency turns into forints at today's rate (fetched daily, editable), and the bank statement is imported and sorted by category, month by month.
3. Honest and repeatable: claims match the app once live rates are built; the screenshots use sample data; anyone can try it from a clone with `node server.js --demo`.
4. Nothing personal on the page.
5. A short, general "before" story, not a personal one.

## Approaches Considered

- A: One-screen pitch. Rejected: it shows features, not the app working.
- B: A month in the life. **Chosen.**
- C: B plus a live converter in the hero. Not chosen for the content; it could still appear as one of Impeccable's `generate` variants.

## Recommended Approach: B, a month in the life

The page follows one student through one month, in the same order as the README demo, so the page and the presentation tell the same story. Every step has one screenshot.

## The page content (draft copy)

### 1. Hero

**Budget Planner**

For international students in Budapest. A scholarship or salary comes in forints, money from home in tögrög, euros or dollars, and Budapest charges in forints. Every income and expense is counted in forints at today's rate, and your bank statement is sorted for you.

Buttons: **Try it in 2 minutes** (jumps to section 5) · **See a month in it** (jumps to section 3)

### 2. The problem

Your scholarship or part-time salary comes in forints, and money from family arrives in tögrög, euros or dollars. Rent, the BKK pass and groceries are in forints. You add it all up at the end of the month with a rate you googled, and only then find out the food money was gone by the 20th.

Budget Planner does the adding up as you go, in forints, and warns you the moment a category goes over.

### 3. A month in it (five steps, one screenshot each)

1. **Set up your month.** Add each income in its own currency: a scholarship or salary in forints, family aid in tögrög, euros or dollars, then the three must-pays: rent, phone, transport. The app shows what is left to plan right away: *170000 left to plan.* (screenshot 01)
2. **Plan, and get warned.** Give each category a plan. Go over it, and a red warning appears: *Food: 10000 over plan.* (screenshot 02)
3. **Every currency in forints.** Today's rates for tögrög, euro and dollar are filled in for you. If your bank's rate differs, type your own. Expenses in any of the four currencies are counted in forints. (screenshot 03, retaken after live rates are built)
4. **Import your bank statement.** Pick the statement .xlsx your bank gives you. The app reads it in your browser, sorts each purchase into Food, Going out or Unknown by the shop's name, puts each month in its own column, and keeps transfers and money in apart from your spending. (screenshots 04 and 06)
5. **Fix a mistake, look back.** Every expense, typed or imported, is in the History with its date and time. Delete a wrong one and the warnings and the report update at once. Pick any earlier month to see what you planned and what was left. (screenshots 08 and 10)

### 4. Your data stays on your computer

- Everything is saved in one file, `budget-data.csv`, next to the app. It opens in Excel.
- The app runs on your own computer only (127.0.0.1); nobody else can reach it.
- Your bank statement file is read in your browser and never uploaded. Only the date, category and amount of each purchase are saved.
- Fetching the exchange rates sends only the currency code "HUF", none of your numbers.

### 5. Try it yourself in 2 minutes

You need Node.js 18 or newer. Nothing else to install.

```
git clone https://github.com/esst-prog2/budget-planner
cd budget-planner
node server.js --demo
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:8766, then import `demo/sample-statement.xlsx`: a made-up statement in a real Mongolian bank's format, with 30 transactions from August to November. The demo always starts from an empty budget.

### 6. How it's built

- Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No frameworks, no dependencies, no build step.
- A small Node server that serves the app and writes the CSV file.
- The .xlsx statement is unzipped in the browser with its built-in DecompressionStream, so there is no spreadsheet library.
- 73 automated tests (`node --test`), including the sample statement import.
- Planned with OpenSpec; every decision is in PLANNING_LOG.md and every step in PROCESS.md.

### 7. What it doesn't do (yet)

The budget is in forints only. Categories are fixed (Food, Clothes, Gym, Subscriptions, Going out, Other, Unknown). No savings goals, no phone notifications.
